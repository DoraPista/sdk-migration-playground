# Story Bank

Brillio and Sage will both ask about your experience. Prepared stories beat improvised ones. Write down six to
eight stories from your own work in the shape below, then check that together they cover the questions at the end.

## The shape

- **Situation**: one sentence. The product, the team, and why it mattered.
- **Task**: what *you* owned.
- **Action**: what you did. Say "I", not "we". Include one technical detail and one decision with its trade-off.
- **Result**: a number if you have one (time saved, crashes down, customers unblocked), and what you learned.

Aim for 90 seconds. Expect *"and then what?"* and know the technical detail one level deeper than you tell it.

## Stories to write

| # | Theme | Job-ad line it proves | To jog your memory | Your story |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A reusable component or library you built | "building SDKs, libraries, or reusable components" | A WPF control or shared service used by several screens, apps or teams. How did you design its API? How did others adopt it? | |
| 2 | A hard production bug | "robust error handling", "attention to detail" | Something you found with logs or dumps. What was the real cause? How did you stop it coming back? | |
| 3 | An async or threading problem | workflows, performance | A frozen UI, a race condition, a deadlock, background work with progress reporting | |

| 8 | A mistake | honesty, learning | Something you shipped that broke. What did you change afterwards? | |
| 9 | Learning something quickly | MAUI, Azure | An unfamiliar technology you got productive in. How did you ramp up? | |

## Which story answers which question

**Brillio (technical and HR):**

- Tell me about yourself: the two-minute intro below.
- Why are you looking for a new role now?
- Why consulting? Why Brillio?
- Your strengths and weaknesses. Use 8 or 9 for the weakness.
- A time you dealt with an unhappy client or stakeholder: 7.
- Where do you see yourself in three to five years?

**Sage:**

- The most complex thing you have built: 1.
- Your hardest bug: 2 or 3.
- Your experience with libraries or SDKs: 1.
- How you make sure your code is good: 2 and 5, plus testing and code review.
- Working with teams who use your code: 1 and 7.
- Your experience with MAUI, .NET Standard or Azure: 9, with an honest level (see below).
- What you would do in your first month.

## Your two-minute intro

1. **Now (20 s):** your current role, the domain, and what you own.
2. **Background (40 s):** years of C# and .NET, years of WPF, and the highlights that match this job (reusable
   components, production debugging, async and multithreading, COM interop).
3. **One proof point (30 s):** story 1 or 2 in one sentence, with its result.
4. **Why this role (30 s):** desktop, SDK and data migration, and why that combination suits you.

## Honest gaps

Have a calm, specific answer ready for the areas you know less well, such as MAUI in production or Azure:

- what you *have* done (the exercises in this repository count: "I've practised resumable uploads against a mock
  platform"),
- how it connects to what you know well (WPF → MAUI, ASP.NET Core → Azure App Service),
- how you would get up to speed in the first weeks.

Nobody expects a mid-level hire to know everything. They do expect you to know where your edges are.
